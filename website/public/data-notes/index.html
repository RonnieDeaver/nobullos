APPENDIX

Data Notes & Definitions

The definitions, formulas, and provenance behind every number this site publishes — versioned, so you can hold us to them.

Version 1.1 · Published August 19, 2026 · Companion to the REE calculator

What this page is

When we publish a number — on this site, in the book, or in the REE calculator — this page records what that number means, how it's computed, and what kind of evidence sits behind it. Each revision is versioned below, so a figure you read today can be checked against the definitions that were in force when it was published.

As of v1.1, NoBull publishes no practice-area benchmarks, medians, or top-quartile comparisons. The calculator prices your levers against your own numbers only. If benchmarks are ever published, their samples and definitions will be versioned on this page first.

Definitions & formulas

Revenue Engine Efficiency (REE)
Revenue for the selected period ÷ Total investment for the selected periodThe score: how many dollars of top-line revenue the engine produces for every $1 invested during the same reporting period. The 3-, 6-, or 12-month choice labels the totals you enter; it does not annualize or extrapolate them.
Total investment
The acquisition and conversion costs counted for the selected period, including ad spend, agency fees, and intake or sales staff costs. Count the same categories every period so comparisons stay meaningful.
Actual revenue basis
Revenue generated entered for the selected periodChoose this basis when you can enter the top-line revenue attributable to the same reporting period. The calculator labels the result Actual revenue.
Estimated revenue basis
Cases × Estimated case valueChoose this basis when you do not have an attributable revenue total. The calculator multiplies the cases entered by your estimated case value and labels the result Estimated revenue.
Cost per Lead (CPL)
Total investment ÷ leadsWhat one lead costs during the selected period.
Lead-to-Consult rate
Consults ÷ leadsThe share of leads that become consults during the selected period.
Consult-to-Case rate
Cases ÷ consultsThe share of consults that become cases during the selected period.
Lead-to-Case rate
Cases ÷ leadsThe whole funnel in one number: the share of leads that become cases.
Cost per Case
Total investment ÷ casesWhat one case costs, all-in, during the selected period. It is unavailable when no cases are entered.
Revenue per Case
Actual revenue ÷ cases, or the entered Estimated case valueThe actual basis derives this figure when at least one case is entered. The estimated basis uses the value you enter directly.

How the 10% improvement values work

The calculator improves one available direct lever at a time by 10%, holds every other input unchanged, and prices the additional top-line revenue for the selected period. Rate improvements are relative: a 40% rate becomes 44%, not 50%.

  • More leads (+10%): additional leads × Lead-to-Case rate × Revenue per Case
  • Lead-to-Consult rate (+10% relative): additional consults × Consult-to-Case rate × Revenue per Case
  • Consult-to-Case rate (+10% relative): additional cases × Revenue per Case
  • Revenue per Case (+10%): cases × the increase in Revenue per Case

Rates stop at 100%, so a rate near that ceiling may receive less than the full lift. A scenario is unavailable when the entered totals cannot support its math, such as actual revenue with no cases to derive Revenue per Case. Values stay in direct funnel order. The output is your arithmetic, never a grade: the calculator makes no judgment about whether a metric is good or bad, and it compares you to no one.

Results disclaimer

No result shown on this site is guaranteed for any individual firm. Outcomes vary based on the firm, its market, practice area, and other relevant circumstances.
